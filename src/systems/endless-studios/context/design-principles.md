## How to use these principles

The principles say what matters most and give the team a shared basis for decisions. Each one names what it puts first when two good options conflict. Then come examples of the principle at work, and the review questions to ask of a screen, flow or piece of copy.

When two principles pull in different directions, the one whose priority applies to the person at risk wins. **Safe before anything** wins over every other principle.

Some examples describe intent rather than the product as it is. Guilds, for one, are planned but not built. Do not present an intended feature as existing.

## 1. Everyone belongs

**You belong, no matter what circumstance.**

Puts the person least sure they belong ahead of the one who already does.

- The first experience is welcoming, not intimidating or lonely.
- Skills celebrate every domain: art, story and production count as much as code.
- Guilds connect people to their own interests. (Intended, not yet built.)
- Works on a shared computer. Identity is per person even when the device is not.

**Review:** Who in the room would feel this is not for them? What tells a non-coder their work counts?

## 2. Learning is not linear

**Join at any level, take your own path.**

Puts the learner's own route ahead of the sequence we planned.

- A clear default path for whoever wants one, and the freedom to leave it.
- Many entry points. Assume people arrive somewhere other than the start.
- The advanced find depth without it getting in the novice's way.
- Work stops without loss and resumes where it left off.

**Review:** Can someone start here without having done what comes before? What happens if they leave halfway?

## 3. Play matters

**Play first, break later.**

Puts curiosity ahead of correctness.

- It invites play before it gives instruction.
- It responds when you touch it.
- Undo is always there. Nothing is scary to try.
- Open-ended. There is no single right output.
- Expressive, not excessive: delight never blocks the task, and the learner's content is the star, not the interface.

**Review:** What can the learner do before reading anything? What is the worst thing that happens if they press the wrong button?

## 4. Share often. Be open.

**Make in the open, never alone.**

Puts peer mentorship ahead of waiting for an expert.

- Rough work goes out early. It's a prototype, not the Mona Lisa.
- Share code, designs, ideas and failures, not just finished things. Learning from each other's mistakes is how the work gets less lonely.
- Every stuck moment has a way to reach a peer first, then a mentor, and they see what the learner sees.
- Errors say what happened and what to try. Never a dead end.

**Review:** Can a learner help another learner here without us in the middle? Where does this flow leave someone stuck with no way out?

## 5. Reflect and refine

**Your story has chapters.**

Puts seeing progress ahead of seeing the finish line.

- Progress is recognised, completion celebrated, the next step prompted.
- Clear beginnings and ends, so you know a stage is done and can look back on it.
- Your history is visible and you can move through it. That is what makes refining possible.

**Review:** How does the learner know a stage is done? Can they get back to what they made last week?

## 6. Create for impact

**Made to be played.**

Puts a game someone plays ahead of a lesson someone completes.

- The test of a learner's game is whether someone plays it.
- Built to be shown: a public portfolio, to friends, family and real audiences.
- Make things you are proud to pass on.

**Review:** Who outside Endless will see or play this? What makes it worth keeping?

## 7. Safe before anything

**Consent, visibility and moderation come before anything reaches or shows a learner under 18. When this conflicts with any other principle, this one wins.**

Puts a learner's safety ahead of every other principle, including their own wish to share.

- A learner without consent still makes and finishes. They just don't publish.
- Code of conduct everywhere people meet.

**Review:** Who can see this, and did someone with the right to decide say yes?

For who the under-18 learners are and what consent involves, see Safeguarding in [User types](user-types.md).
